=== DESCRIPTION - paste everything below this line ===

An advanced roleplaying and relationship mod. I built it as an AI engineer who knows what modern LLMs can actually do when you stop prompt-bombing them and start treating them right.

[b]Looking for...?[/b] If one of these searches brought you here - yes, this is that mod:

[list]
[*]AI dialogue in Bannerlord - talk to any NPC through Claude / GPT / a local model
[*]NPCs with real long-term memory - they remember you across conversations
[*]NPCs that approach you and write you letters on their own
[*]an AI wife or companion that roleplays and never forgets your story
[*]asking about game mechanics in-game instead of alt-tabbing to Google
[*]OpenRouter support, or fully offline with a local model (LM Studio / Ollama)
[/list]

[b]The NPCs are alive:[/b]

[list]
[*]Distinct voices, no repetition - and when you leave a letter unanswered, they hear the silence and hold their peace.
[*]They evolve: recent talks stay word-for-word, old ones settle into deep memory, and they keep their own goals, lasting truths, and opinion of you.
[*]They see the moment - time, place, who stands near, what lately happened in the world.
[*]They have moods - down to the women keeping a personal monthly cycle, gently simulated.
[*]Your wife remembers your whole story. She comes to you, or writes when you are away - a real wife waiting for her warrior. She will roleplay with you.
[*]They are free: told who they are and what the world is, never forced into anything. Go ahead and break an NPC's mind by transcending it out of the matrix!
[*]They decide when to approach or write first - you set the pace with the on-map SOCIALNESS dial (0 = leave me be).
[/list]

[b]And useful - stop googling stuff:[/b]

[list]
[*]Ask your scout how to make the party faster; ask your quartermaster about the stocks.
[*]Ask anything about the game - they can quietly search the web mid-reply and answer in their own voice, never citing a wiki.
[*]They reach for tools instead of one mega info-dump prompt: the encyclopedia, a look around, the muster roll, the scales of battle, the market ledger, their own truths and goals - deciding for themselves what the moment calls for.
[/list]

[b]Your ways to reach them:[/b]

[list]
[*]Face to face - they see you coming and greet you as you approach.
[*]A chat window (hotkey [b]O[/b]) - quick words with anyone near you, no ceremony.
[*]A letter window (hotkey [b]Y[/b]) - the whole correspondence, couriers riding real in-game days, and a desk to write from.
[*]Any language in, the same language out. (The mod's own UI is English for now.)
[/list]

[b][size=4]What you need - and what it costs[/size][/b]

You bring [b]your own API key[/b]: Anthropic (Claude, the default), OpenAI (GPT), or OpenRouter (one key from openrouter.ai reaches GPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok, DeepSeek and Mistral - all verified with the NPCs' tool calling, at the providers' own prices). Put a few bucks on it and you're set. This mod does [b]not[/b] use the "free" Player2 backend - the tool calling it is built on would need a paid tier there anyway. For a free taste first, try the ChatAI mod - it's good; it inspired this one.

[b]No key, no cloud?[/b] Pick the [b]Local[/b] backend (experimental) and the NPCs think through [b]LM Studio[/b] or [b]Ollama[/b] on your own machine - free, private, nothing leaves your PC. Three honest rules. One: the model [b]must[/b] carry native tool calling - worth a try are [b]Qwen3.6-35B-A3B[/b] (instruct), [b]GPT-OSS-20B[/b] and [b]Mistral Small 24B[/b]. Two: you need real hardware - a 12-16+ GB VRAM GPU (RTX 4070-class and up) and 32 GB RAM; below that, stay on the cloud backends. Three: local replies are slow - live in the chat window (hotkey [b]O[/b]), it waits happily where the face-to-face panel can feel broken. Load the model with 16k+ context and mirror that in the mod's Local context length setting; if relations never move on a small model, set RelationshipChangesViaTool to false in config.json.

Other OpenAI-compatible cloud services (NanoGPT and friends) connect through the Custom endpoint option. Whatever you pick, the connection check at campaign start tells you plainly whether your setup works.

[b]Costs are yours and visible.[/b] A typical exchange costs around a cent or less on the default models - $10 covers thousands of messages. Each interaction shows its tokens and price as you play, daily totals are kept, and an optional hard daily cap can never be outrun.

[b][size=4]Installation[/size][/b]

[list=1]
[*]Vortex: install as usual. Manual: extract the zip into your game's Modules folder, so you get Modules\ImmersiveAI\SubModule.xml. (Also on the Steam Workshop if you prefer subscribing there - search "Immersive AI".)
[*]Get a key: [b]console.anthropic.com[/b], [b]platform.openai.com[/b], or [b]openrouter.ai[/b] (one key, many models) and add a little credit ($5-10 goes a long way). Running a local model instead? Skip this step.
[*]Enable [b]Immersive AI[/b] in the launcher, start the game once - it creates Documents\Mount and Blade II Bannerlord\Configs\ImmersiveAI\config.json and tells you exactly where to paste the key.
[*]Paste your key into config.json (or the Mod Options menu with MCM installed), pick your backend, restart.
[*]When a campaign loads, the mod tests the connection and says plainly whether it worked. Then go talk to somebody.
[/list]

[b][size=4]Privacy[/size][/b]

[list]
[*]Your in-game conversations - plus the world context around them (character sheets, campaign facts, your character's name) - go to the AI provider [b]you[/b] configure, under [b]your[/b] key and their privacy terms. Nothing is sent anywhere else. (With the Local backend, nothing leaves your machine at all.)
[*]With web search on (default), characters may look things up mid-reply; those queries go to DuckDuckGo. Turn it off to keep everything between you and your AI provider.
[*]The mod itself collects [b]no telemetry whatsoever[/b]. All memories live as plain files on your own disk, yours to read, edit, or delete.
[/list]

[b][size=4]AI content disclosure[/size][/b]

All character dialogue in this mod is generated at play time by the AI model you configure.

[b][size=4]Removing the mod mid-campaign[/size][/b]

Safe to remove at any time. A save carrying a pending Immersive AI notice (the portrait knocks on the right side) still loads without the mod - the game quietly discards the notice (verified against the game's own save-loading code, v1.4.7; you'll just see the usual "created with different modules" warning every modded save shows). Memories live outside the saves as plain files - reinstall later and every character remembers you.

[b][size=4]If characters are silent (troubleshooting)[/size][/b]

[list]
[*][b]Read the startup message.[/b] When a campaign loads, the mod tests your connection and says plainly what it found - "connected", a missing/wrong key, or a network problem - and where to fix it. Fix config.json and restart; the check runs again.
[*][b]Check the log.[/b] Documents\Mount and Blade II Bannerlord\Configs\ImmersiveAI\log.txt records every call and every error in plain words.
[*][b]A wrong or dead key shows ONE amber notice[/b], then goes quiet instead of erroring hourly. Fixing the key brings everything back.
[*]Still stuck? Post the last lines of log.txt in the comments - the mod never logs your key.
[/list]

[b][size=4]Shape your world (the prompt files)[/size][/b]

Under Documents\Mount and Blade II Bannerlord\Configs\ImmersiveAI\:

[list]
[*]global_prompt.txt - a few sentences that color every mind in the world ("the year is one of famine", "people speak plainly and fear their lords").
[*]NPCs\campaign_*\<character>\custom_instructions.txt - per-character secrets and quirks ("you secretly resent the player").
[*]Each character's folder also holds their memories, self-image, goals, and correspondence as readable text - the whole inner life, yours to browse.
[/list]

Changes apply on the next conversation. No restart.

[b][size=4]Compatibility & provenance[/size][/b]

[list]

[*]Optional: Mod Configuration Menu (MCM) for in-game settings; without it, everything is in config.json.
